References and conventions

The bibliography for this study, and a statement of the rules it is written under. Every entry below has had its bibliographic record checked against an independent index; entries whose full text has not been read are marked as such, because a citation is a claim and claims should carry their provenance.

Conventions used throughout

  1. Every symbol is defined where it first appears, with SI units. The complete list is in the glossary, which also expands every abbreviation.
  2. Displayed equations are numbered so they can be referred to without ambiguity.
  3. Every claim is labelled by type. Derived here, assumed, taken from the literature, or measured. Where a number is assumed, the assumption is stated rather than buried.
  4. Numerical results carry their provenance. If a figure was produced by a script in this repository, the script is named in the caption so the result can be regenerated.
  5. Approximations are named at the point of use, together with the regime in which they fail.
  6. Nothing is reproduced from a source. Derivations are worked in full and written in the author's own words; where a result is standard, its originator is cited rather than paraphrased silently.
A limitation stated openly. The bibliographic details below were verified against independent indexes, but most of these papers sit behind paywalls and their full texts have not been read for this study. Where a result is attributed to a source, that attribution reflects the standard scholarly record of who first derived it, not a claim to have checked the original derivation line by line. Entries marked full text consulted are the exceptions. Manufacturing a plausible-looking citation is worse than admitting this, so it is admitted.

Guided-wave theory

  1. D. Gloge, "Weakly guiding fibers," Applied Optics 10(10), 2252–2258 (1971). The origin of the LP mode set and of the weak-guidance approximation used in step 2 of the bend derivation. Record verified via NASA ADS.
  2. D. Marcuse, "Loss analysis of single-mode fiber splices," Bell System Technical Journal 56(5), 703–718 (1977). Source of both the Gaussian mode-size fit (w/a = 0.65 + 1.619V−3/2 + 2.879V−6) and the lateral, angular and longitudinal misalignment loss formulas. Record verified via Wiley; full text openly available at the Internet Archive.
  3. D. Marcuse, "Curvature loss formula for optical fibers," Journal of the Optical Society of America 66(3), 216–220 (1976). The closed-form bend loss of a step-index fiber, and the source of the exponent (2/3)γ³R/β² recovered independently in step 11. Record verified via Optica Publishing Group.
  4. E. A. J. Marcatili, "Bends in optical dielectric guides," Bell System Technical Journal 48(7), 2103–2132 (1969). The other founding treatment of waveguide bends. Record verified via Wiley; full text openly available at the Internet Archive.
  5. M. Heiblum and J. H. Harris, "Analysis of curved optical waveguides by conformal transformation," IEEE Journal of Quantum Electronics QE-11(2), 75–83 (1975). Full text consulted. The conformal transformation of step 6, and the treatment of transition loss where a straight guide meets a curved one. Open copy at the author's institution.

Textbooks

  1. R. P. Feynman, R. B. Leighton and M. Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Vol. I, Addison-Wesley (1963). Full text consulted. Ch. 23 for the driven oscillator, Ch. 30 and 31 for the origin of the refractive index by re-radiation. Openly available from Caltech at feynmanlectures.caltech.edu.

Glass waveguides and co-packaged optics

  1. L. Brusberg et al., "Glass Substrate for Co-Packaged Optics," Proc. IMAPS 2022, pp. 236–241. Full text consulted. Source of the reported ion-exchange waveguide performance: mode-field matched to Corning SMF-28 Ultra at 1310 nm, propagation loss below 0.1 dB/cm, and fiber coupling loss of 0.3 dB with air cladding against 0.1 dB with index-matched adhesive. Open PDF.
  2. L. Brusberg et al., "Glass Substrate With Integrated Waveguides for Surface Mount Photonic Packaging," Journal of Lightwave Technology (2021). Full text consulted. Open PDF.

Numerical methods

  1. M. Abramowitz and I. A. Stegun (eds.), Handbook of Mathematical Functions, National Bureau of Standards Applied Mathematics Series 55 (1964). Source of the polynomial approximations for J₀, J₁, I₀, I₁, K₀ and K₁ implemented in fiber-mode.html, sections 9.4 and 9.8.
  2. Corning Incorporated, SMF-28 Ultra Optical Fiber Product Information. Source of the fiber parameters used throughout: core diameter 8.2 µm, numerical aperture 0.14, mode field diameter 10.4 µm at 1550 nm and 9.2 µm at 1310 nm, cable cutoff at or below 1260 nm. The internal inconsistency between the quoted numerical aperture and the other two specifications is analysed in stage 1.
