Three things to do with it

  1. Find the cutoff. Drag the wavelength down and watch V cross 2.405. With the datasheet NA of 0.14 this happens near 1500 nm, which would mean the fiber is multimode at 1310. It is not, so the quoted NA cannot be the one that governs the mode.
  2. Find the real NA, twice. Slide NA until cutoff sits at 1260 nm, the specified cable cutoff. Then return to 1550 nm and slide NA until the Marcuse MFD reads 10.4 µm. The two routes use independent datasheet numbers and should land within a few percent of each other, near 0.118.
  3. Watch where the light lives. Compare power in the cladding at 1310 nm and at 1550 nm. Same core in both cases. This is why the same fiber bends worse at the longer wavelength.
Method: the eigenvalue equation u·J₁(u)/J₀(u) = w·K₁(w)/K₀(w) with u² + w² = V² is solved by bisection, using the Abramowitz and Stegun polynomial approximations for the Bessel functions. The field is J₀(ur/a) in the core and K₀(wr/a) in the cladding, matched at the boundary. Power fractions are integrated numerically. Note that the "exact 1/e" readout uses the 1/e field definition, which is not the Petermann II definition the datasheet uses; use the Marcuse column when comparing against a specification.
