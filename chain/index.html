From fiber to ASIC: the optical chain

An explainer that follows a single photon path through a co-packaged optics assembly, one stage at a time, from the fiber that delivers the light to the point where it stops being light at all. Every stage is built around experiments you can re-run yourself in a browser or on your own machine.

A co-packaged optics package is usually drawn as a single arrow from a fiber to a chip. That arrow hides five different physical regimes, four different classes of simulation tool, and at least three places where the light can be thrown away by a micrometre of misplacement.

This study walks the path in order. Each stage answers three questions: what is physically happening, which equations describe it and where they come from, and which tool you would actually reach for to simulate it. The last question matters more than it sounds. Ray tracing cannot model a single-mode waveguide, and a mode solver cannot model a lens relay, so half of the skill is knowing which half of the toolbox a given problem belongs to.

The stages

Foundations
Rays, lenses and aberration
Where light can be treated as rays, and where that stops working. Focal length by experiment, the thick-lens correction, and spherical aberration measured with a ruler.
Stage 1
The fiber, and what it delivers
Why a 0.45% index step traps light, why only one mode survives, how wide that mode really is, and why the datasheet and the textbook disagree by 11%.
Stage 2
Waveguides in the glass
Ion exchange as a diffusion process, why the index contrast has to be small, how lateral diffusion decides the shape, and what the joint with the fiber costs, why bend loss is tunnelling, and why that makes the glass a board rather than a chip.
Stage 3
Glass to PIC
Adiabatic evanescent transfer across the bondline: phase matching, supermodes, beat length, and why the taper tip width is a design variable. Not yet written.
Stage 4
Inside the PIC
Routing, polarization, wavelength multiplexing, modulation and detection. Not yet written.
Stage 5
The O/E boundary
Where photons become electrons and the optical chain ends. Not yet written.

Which tool belongs to which stage

Worth keeping in view from the start, because it is the question that decides whether a simulation is meaningful or merely produced.

Note that Zemax appears only in the foundations row and, later, wherever a design expands the beam into free space. The rest of the chain belongs to the Lumerical family of tools and their open-source equivalents. Two different skills that are often written on a job advertisement as one word.

How to use this

Each stage carries the exercises that produced its numbers. They are written so they can be repeated from scratch, with nothing but a browser for the early ones and Python for the later ones. The measured values shown are real results from running them, not illustrations, so if you repeat an exercise and get something different, one of the two runs is wrong and it is worth finding out which.

Each stage also ends with questions whose answers are folded away. They are there to be attempted before being opened.
